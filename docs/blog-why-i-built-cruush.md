# Why I built cruush and want people to own their technology

Opening other apps as a trans man can feel like being a slab of meat thrown to the dogs. I’m mostly there for other trans people, and I prefer women. I receive rape threats within half an hour of opening cruising apps and leave feeling depressed and turned off. I can’t imagine how much unwanted attention femmes face. I have no problem with straight people using cruush; the issue is privacy from harassment and unwanted attention. In my view, the value of these communities comes from queer people’s bodies and participation. Platforms need us, and we deserve options that respect us. I built cruush because I was tired of opening a cruising app hoping to find someone and receiving rape threats instead. I was tired of being unable to stop cis men from seeing my profile when that was what I needed to feel safe.

I wanted connection, pleasure, and control over who could reach me. I kept finding myself in systems that made me feel exposed and disposable.

I was also tired of watching people in my community struggle with app designs that seemed to encourage compulsive checking and harmful patterns of behavior. Advertising presented as “taboo” could make dangerous behavior feel ordinary. I felt that our vulnerability was being used to keep us engaged, while we became the product being sold.

I wanted another option. I wanted us to be able to cruise virtually without needing straight people's permission.

That is why I made cruush.

I don’t need dark patterns to give cruush value. I believe I’m building a better human experience, and its value comes from the features and the community. I want people to return because they want to connect, not because the design pressures them to keep checking.

I won’t allow harmful “taboo” promotions or open drug markets on cruush. I don’t want the app to encourage behavior that harms people. I’m not interested in policing consensual DMs about drug use, and I don’t want anyone confronted with triggering content without their consent. Those values should shape how people experience the app.

In my view, weak moderation and designs that time advertisements or deals around repeated logins can turn our community’s vulnerability into revenue. I believe companies can end up profiting from queer people’s addictions, including companies controlled by people outside our community. I’m concerned about cruising platforms where promotions of animal sexual abuse, incest, or sex involving minors appear. Those are forms of content I want excluded. A service should help people connect, not make money from harm.

I want to make software that is actually worth your money. Helping people connect is a beautiful service. For cruush, that is what I want to earn money from, alongside original content and useful features. I want cruising to feel fun and magical. I want people to leave feeling good about themselves, with dignity and joy in queer sex.

I want cruush to be an app you feel cool being seen on. I fully support discreet users, and privacy should always be available. Being visible should be a choice, too. You don’t have to treat queer sex as dirty or hide it if you don’t want to.

I was inspired by queer parties at BASEMENT, where I saw couples of different genders and sexualities having sex under the same roof. I’ve seen that shared community work in real life. That experience convinced me that a cruising app could bring people together across genders and sexualities, too. I want to help create that feeling of belonging online.

To me, cruush needed camp to feel truly queer. That is why I wrote its lore: a parody of satanic ritual panic and superstition, reclaiming the harmful names and meanings attached to queer desire. I want to turn those labels into something playful, theatrical, and ours. The lore gives the Grimoire a world to grow inside.

I lead the branding and the app’s look, with commissioned custom logo and icon work and some visuals I drew myself in Figma. I want software that looks like a designer made it, with intention, personality, and magic. I refuse to make a boring-looking queer app.

## The three products

**[cruush](https://technosovereign.com/work/cruush)** is a queer social and cruising app for connecting with nearby people. I’ve built custom messaging, location privacy, automated moderation, and payment infrastructure around the community I want it to serve.

**[sovereignpay](https://technosovereign.com/work/sovereignpay)** is the cryptocurrency payment processor I built for cruush, developed into a product for legal businesses that want to accept cryptocurrency directly into their own wallets without bank underwriting for card processing. Its software includes payment links, invoices, transaction tracking, and merchant-wallet payments.

**[HEKA](https://technosovereign.com/work/hekatonomous)** started with a practical frustration: I didn’t want to keep paying for Copilot. I wanted an experience like Codex that could run with a local model. I built an original agent product around that goal, with persistent tasks, computer control, voice, and remote interfaces. You can use local models or your own API tokens without a HEKA subscription. The public release will focus on 24/7 GitHub issue-to-fix workflows, with each step reviewed using multiple models and up to 100 subagents.

HEKA and sovereignpay are built; their consumer releases are the next step.

I want to build an open-source accessibility agent from HEKA’s codebase for blind users. It would interpret screenshots with a small local model, describe what’s on screen, and use voice commands to navigate different interfaces without requiring special accessibility markup or a screen-reader integration.

My goal is to make more of the internet, apps and some games accessible, including interfaces that are difficult to use with conventional screen readers. I intend to develop and test these capabilities with blind users. The tool is still unnamed.

## Labyrinths and Parties

I believe Labyrinths can become cruush’s defining feature. Grindr is known for its [grid](https://help.grindr.com/hc/en-us/articles/12155355365011-The-Grid), and Sniffies for its [map](https://sniffieshush.com/blogs/articles/cruising-tips-from-the-founder). I want people to think of cruush and think of Labyrinths.

Labyrinths are branching encounters where two people shape one shared story through their choices. The first version will be text-based. Players set private boundaries before entering and can leave at any time. I’m developing personalized stories shaped by approved profile details and preferences, with random matching, invitations to someone, and a private solo mode. Later, I want illustrated scenes.

The Grimoire is a further narrative direction: an ongoing weekly visual novel based on the lore of cruush. You would play as one of two characters drawn from a larger recurring cast, and explore two points of view of the same scene. Their stories would continue across weekly chapters, with different routes and endings. I’m working with an illustrator to develop this concept. This is the narrative and visual experience I want to develop alongside the personalized text Labyrinths.

The local coded prototype already demonstrates branching text, private limits, and turn progression. Social pairing and delivery are simulated in that preview. The broader personalized engine and continuing chapters are still in development.

## Privacy and ownership

My privacy principle: **What happens in cruush, stays on cruush.**

I will never sell cruush, even for a trillion dollars.

I’m its only administrator, and I keep database access under my own control. I’m the only person with full server access. My phone and fingerprint are part of how I protect that access. I work for myself; no employer can fire me from this work, and I won’t let a board push me out of cruush. I’m the only person I trust with this server. They’ll have to rip this key from my cold, dead hands. I self-host cruush with European-owned Hetzner; our current server is in Virginia. The European infrastructure I’m planning is part of my effort to protect the community and reduce dependence on American infrastructure. I don’t trust the future of this database to someone whose priorities might be different from mine.

I designed the database around making the collection of member information difficult. Sensitive content, including messages, is encrypted on the server. The current system still allows authorized server access for operation and moderation. We’re exploring end-to-end encryption as a further step toward the independence I want.

I will never sell your data or use it to train AI. That commitment is part of why cruush exists. I’ve already made decisions that put my values ahead of profit, and I intend to keep doing that for as long as I’m responsible for cruush.

## The final goal is an off grid cruush

My final goal is a version of cruush that works without a central server or a company deciding whether we are allowed to connect.

I want queer people to have a way to find one another even when they cannot safely talk about it on the public internet at all. Where ordinary cruising services are blocked or inaccessible, I want there to be another option.

We’re exploring local communication through a network of nodes, including [Meshtastic](https://meshtastic.org/docs/introduction/). Meshtastic uses compatible radio hardware to relay messages without conventional internet infrastructure. I want to connect that kind of network to devices running cruush, so nearby people could communicate during a blackout or when normal access is censored.

The network would still need powered devices and compatible links. The version I’m describing has to be developed and tested. I am committed to building this sovereign version within my lifetime. The goal is for it to keep working independently of me, any future operator, or a central server. Even if a future operator takes the main service in a direction I reject, I want the community to retain an independent version it can keep using. I want this technology to outlast its creator.

A spark in the dark. That is what I want cruush to become.

There is no hidden plan to sell the community out. I want to make better software because our community deserves a real choice.
